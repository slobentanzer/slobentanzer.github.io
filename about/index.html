About

Lab Website: slolab.ai

I am a pharmacologist turned research software engineer, working on the reliable automation of science with modern artificial intelligence. I pursue this as a Principal Investigator at the Institute of Computational Biology at Helmholtz Center Munich since 2025, where I am also a Helmholtz AI Young Investigator. I lead the Computational Biology Unit at the German Center for Diabetes Research, and I am affiliated with the Open Targets group at the European Bioinformatics Institute (EMBL-EBI) since 2024 and the department Internal Medicine 1 at Heidelberg University Hospital since 2026.

Dealing with the avalanche of biomedical measurements without throwing away large parts of the data requires smart digital infrastructure. To this end I develop BioCypher, a unified framework for biomedical knowledge representation that automates harmonisation across a multitude of research tasks. Building on this infrastructure, the BioChatter project integrates Large Language Models into the biomedical research workflow, with the aim of increasing accessibility of biomedical knowledge and methods.

My current work centers on agentic AI for science: the governance and empirical evaluation of agentic systems in biomedical research. I develop FAIR-compliant frameworks for the prospective design of agentic deployments (the Agentic Automation Canvas) and methods for measuring whether these systems deliver on their promises. I advise German federal agencies and European bodies on frontier AI, and chair task forces on agentic systems at ELIXIR Europe, RAISE/SCIANCE, and Helmholtz AI.

I have received funding from the DFG, Open Targets, Helmholtz AI, and the Federal Institute for Public Health to develop a new generation of biomedical knowledge management systems, agentic AI infrastructure, and evaluation of AI in public health. Much of this work goes into building and supporting the open-source community around these tools.

Short bio in third person

Dr. Sebastian Lobentanzer is a Principal Investigator at Helmholtz Munich, a Helmholtz AI Young Investigator, and head of the Computational Biology Unit at the German Center for Diabetes Research (DZD), where he runs his lab for Accessible Biomedical AI Research (https://slolab.ai).

He is affiliated with the department of Internal Medicine 1 at Heidelberg University Hospital and the Open Targets group at the European Bioinformatics Institute (EMBL-EBI). He trained as a pharmacist, completed a PhD in pharmacology and toxicology, and was a postdoctoral researcher in the lab of Julio Saez-Rodriguez at Heidelberg University Hospital from 2021 to 2025.

His research now asks how science can be automated reliably. He came to this question from pharmacology and knowledge representation: he created BioCypher, an open-source ecosystem for biomedical knowledge graphs, and BioChatter, a platform for applying large language models to biomedical research (https://biocypher.org). Both address the same underlying problem: representing what we know so that machines can reason over it without distorting it.

His current work centers on agentic AI for science, systems that plan and execute parts of the research process. He develops methods for their prospective design and governance and for measuring whether they deliver what they promise. He advises German federal agencies and European bodies on frontier AI and chairs task forces on agentic systems at ELIXIR Europe, RAISE/SCIANCE, and Helmholtz AI.
